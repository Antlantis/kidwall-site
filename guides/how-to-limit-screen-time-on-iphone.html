How to limit screen time on your child's iPhone

Updated November 3, 2026 · Steps checked against Apple Support pages published October 26, 2026

Every iPhone already comes with free parental controls: Apple's Screen Time. You can use it to set daily time limits, schedule device-free hours, block apps and filter websites. This guide walks through it step by step, using Apple's own instructions.

Which steps do I follow? Apple redesigned parental controls in iOS 27. The redesign brings Time Allowances and Screen Time Schedules. If every device in your family is on iOS 27, use Part 2. If some devices can't update, you keep the older Screen Time with Downtime and App Limits, so use Part 3. To check, go to Settings › General › About.

1. Before you start: Family Sharing and a Child Account

Apple recommends managing your child's Screen Time from your own iPhone, not theirs. For that you need:

  1. Family Sharing set up on your iPhone.
  2. A Child Account (an Apple Account of their own) for your child in your Family Sharing group. Apple stresses that your child shouldn't share an account with anyone else.
  3. All devices in the family updated to the latest software, so Screen Time settings sync between them.

Then set up Screen Time for your child:

  1. Open the Settings app on your iPhone.
  2. Tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  3. Tap Screen Time, then follow the onscreen instructions.

Source: Apple: Use Screen Time to manage your child's iPhone or iPad.

Upgrading to the new parental controls (iOS 27)

To switch on the redesigned controls, every device a parent, guardian or managed child used in the last 90 days must be on version 27 of iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS or visionOS.

  1. See which devices still need updating: Settings › Family › View Family Devices.
  2. Update each device: Settings › General › Software Update.
  3. Then go to Settings › Family and tap Get Started to transfer your existing controls or set up new ones.

Devices that can't update show up under Family › View Family Devices › Legacy Devices. They keep working with the older Screen Time settings. Source: Apple: Update your family devices to iOS 27.

2. iOS 27: Time Allowances, Schedules and blocked apps

All of these start on your iPhone in Settings › Family › your child's name. Source for this section: Apple: Set up parental controls to manage your child's iPhone or iPad.

Set a daily time budget (Time Allowances)

Time Allowances limit how long your child can use apps in categories like entertainment, games and social media. You can set one daily budget across all categories and then adjust individual categories.

  1. Open Settings, tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Time Allowances, tap the Add button, then follow the onscreen instructions.

Choose when apps are available (Screen Time Schedules)

Schedules control which apps your child can use at different times of day, like before school, during school and after school. You can set separate routines for weekdays and weekends. You can also pause device use, allow unlimited use, or change today's schedule from the Screen Time settings.

  1. Open Settings, tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Screen Time Schedule, then turn on Schedule.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add Schedule.
  4. Select the days the schedule applies to.

Block a specific app or website

  1. Open Settings, tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Apps & Websites. Enter your Screen Time passcode if asked.
  3. Select the app or website, tap Block App or Block Website, then tap again to confirm.

A blocked app isn't deleted. It's dimmed on the Home Screen, and you can unblock it at any time.

Filter websites

  1. Open Settings, tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Apps & Websites, then Restrictions, then Filtering under Websites.
  3. Choose Limit Adult Websites, or Approved Websites Only to use Ask to Browse, where your child has to ask before visiting a new site.

Ask to Browse is on by default for children under 13, and Limit Adult Websites is on by default for children under 18. Ages vary by country or region.

Approve downloads and purchases (Ask to Buy)

  1. Open Settings, tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Apps & Websites, then Restrictions.
  3. Tap Ask to Buy, then turn on Require Purchase Approval.

3. iOS 26 and earlier: Downtime and App Limits

If your family is still on the older Screen Time, start from Settings › Screen Time on your iPhone and tap your child's name. Source for this section: Apple iPhone User Guide (iOS 26): Set schedules with Screen Time.

Schedule Downtime (for example, bedtime)

  1. Go to Settings › Screen Time, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap App & Website Activity and turn it on if you haven't already.
  3. Tap Downtime, then tap Scheduled. To start right now, tap Turn On Downtime Until Tomorrow instead.
  4. Choose Every Day or Customize Days, for example a later time on weekends, then set the start and end times.

During Downtime, only calls, messages and the apps you allow are available. Your child gets a reminder 5 minutes before Downtime begins.

Make Downtime actually block apps

By default, Screen Time limits can be ignored once they're reached. To make Downtime a hard stop, first set a Screen Time passcode (see section 4), then:

  1. Go to Settings › Screen Time, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Downtime, then turn on Block at Downtime.

Restricted apps then appear dimmed with an hourglass icon and can't be opened.

Set daily App Limits

  1. Go to Settings › Screen Time, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap App Limits, then tap Add Limit.
  3. Select one or more categories, like Games or Social Networking. Tap a category's name to pick individual apps instead. If you select several, one limit covers them all.
  4. Tap Next, then set the amount of time allowed. Tap Customize Days to set different limits on different days.
  5. Tap Add to finish.

Choose apps and contacts that are always allowed

Go to Settings › Screen Time › your child's name › Always Allowed to keep certain apps and contacts available, even during Downtime. Apple gives emergencies as an example.

4. Lock it down with a Screen Time passcode

A Screen Time passcode stops your child from changing the settings. If you manage your child's account through Family Sharing, Apple says the family organizer or a parent/guardian must set the child's Screen Time passcode on their own device, not the child's. Pick a code your child doesn't know and that's different from your iPhone's unlock code. Source: Apple Support.

5. Tips that make limits stick

  • Start generous, then tighten. A limit your child can live with is easier to enforce than a sudden drop.
  • Agree on the rules together and explain what happens when time runs out.
  • Always allow the essentials, like Phone, Messages with family, and anything needed for school.
  • Check the games' own settings too. Many popular games and chat apps have their own parent controls for chat and purchases that Screen Time doesn't cover.
