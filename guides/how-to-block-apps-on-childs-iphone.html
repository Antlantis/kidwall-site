How to block TikTok, YouTube, Roblox or any app on your child's iPhone

Updated November 4, 2026 · Steps checked against Apple, TikTok, YouTube and Roblox help pages

You don't need to take the phone away or buy anything to block one app. Apple's free Screen Time can block a specific app, stop your child from installing it again, and block the website version too. This guide covers all three, plus the parent controls built into TikTok, YouTube and Roblox for when you'd rather allow the app with limits.

Which steps do I follow? Apple redesigned parental controls in iOS 27. If your family has upgraded to the new controls, use Part 2. If you still see Downtime and App Limits under Settings › Screen Time, you're on the older controls, so use Part 3. Either way you need Family Sharing and a Child Account set up first, and a Screen Time passcode your child doesn't know.

1. Block, limit, or allow with controls?

  • Block it completely if the app isn't right for your child yet. Screen Time can block the app, block new installs, and block its website.
  • Limit it if the problem is how long, not whether. See our guide to limiting screen time for Time Allowances (iOS 27) and App Limits (iOS 26).
  • Allow it with the app's own controls if it's mostly fine but you want a say in content, messages or spending. Screen Time controls the iPhone, but it can't change settings inside TikTok, YouTube or Roblox. Those have their own parent tools (see Part 4).

2. iOS 27: block an app, stop reinstalls, block the website

All of these start on your iPhone in Settings › Family › your child's name. Sources for this section: Apple: Set up parental controls and Apple: Block or allow access to apps and websites.

Block a specific app

  1. Open Settings, tap Family, then tap your child's name.
  2. Tap Apps & Websites. Enter your Screen Time passcode if asked.
  3. Select the app, tap Block App, then tap again to confirm. You can also tap the Add button and type the app's name.

Blocking doesn't delete the app or its data. Apple says a blocked app is dimmed on the Home Screen (its child safety guide says hidden) until you allow it again. Everything you've blocked shows up in the Blocked tab, so you can unblock it later.

Stop it from being installed or deleted

Blocking one app doesn't stop your child from finding a similar one. To control installs:

  • Ask to Buy: Apps & Websites › Restrictions › Ask to Buy, then turn on Require Purchase Approval. Your child has to send you a request before downloading an eligible app, and you approve or decline it from your own phone. In the US it's on by default for children under 13.
  • Block installing entirely: Apps & Websites › App Store › Installing Apps › Blocked.
  • Block deleting apps: the same Restrictions screen controls whether apps can be deleted (Apple's "If you can't delete an app" page calls it Deleting Apps). Parents have reported kids deleting and reinstalling a game to reset its time limit, so it's worth setting this to Blocked if you use limits.
  • Limit by age rating: Content & Privacy Restrictions, then pick a rating for apps under Content Restrictions. Your child only sees apps rated for their age group.

Block the website version too

TikTok, YouTube and Roblox all work in a web browser, so blocking the app alone can leave a gap.

  1. In Settings › Family › your child's name, tap Apps & Websites.
  2. Select the website (or tap Add and enter it, for example tiktok.com), then tap Block Website.

For tighter control, go to Apps & Websites › Restrictions › Filtering and choose Approved Websites Only (Ask to Browse), so your child has to ask before visiting any new site. Apple says Ask to Browse works in Safari and other supported browser apps.

3. iOS 26 and earlier: the older Screen Time

On the older controls, start from Settings › Screen Time on your iPhone and tap your child's name. Sources for this section: Apple iPhone User Guide (iOS 26): Block apps, app downloads, websites, and purchases, Set up parental controls with Family Sharing and Set schedules with Screen Time.

Block apps by age rating

  1. Go to Settings › Screen Time, tap your child's name, then tap Content & Privacy Restrictions and turn it on.
  2. Tap App Store, Media, Web, & Games, then Apps.
  3. Choose an age rating (for example 9+), or Don't Allow to block apps regardless of age.

If you also use Ask to Buy, your child can ask for an exception for an app above their rating. Approved exceptions are listed in the same Apps screen, where you can remove them.

Limit a single app

To control one third-party app, use App Limits: Settings › Screen Time › your child's name › App Limits › Add Limit. Tap a category name to pick the individual app, tap Next, then set the time allowed. By default, Screen Time limits can be ignored once reached, so set a Screen Time passcode to make them stick.

Turn off built-in apps

For Apple's own apps, like Safari or Camera: Content & Privacy Restrictions › Allowed Apps & Features, then turn off the ones you want to restrict. Apple says they aren't deleted, just hidden from the Home Screen until you turn them back on.

Control downloads and purchases

Content & Privacy Restrictions › iTunes & App Store Purchases sets restrictions for store purchases and redownloads.

Block a website

  1. Content & Privacy Restrictions › App Store, Media, Web, & Games › Web Content.
  2. Choose the option that limits adult websites, then tap Add Website under Never Allow and enter the address.

4. Use the app's own parent controls

If you'd rather allow the app with guardrails, these are set inside each app, not in iPhone Settings.

TikTok: Family Pairing

Family Pairing links your TikTok account to your teen's. You can set their daily screen time from your own account (TikTok sets it to one hour by default for teens 13 to 17), schedule time away, filter their feed by keyword, and decide who can message them. To set it up: Profile › Menu (☰) › Settings and privacy › Family Pairing › Continue, choose Parent or Teen, then follow the steps. Source: TikTok Support: Family Pairing.

YouTube: YouTube Kids, supervised accounts and Family Center

YouTube offers three options. YouTube Kids is a separate app for kids under 13. A supervised kid account, managed through Google's Family Link, moves under-13s onto regular YouTube with content settings you choose. A supervised teen account links your account to your teen's in Family Center, so you can set content settings, take-a-break and bedtime reminders, and a limit on Shorts scrolling. In the YouTube app: You › Settings › Family Center › Invite a teen. Both of you have to agree, and either one can turn supervision off. Sources: YouTube Help: Supervised teen account and Google For Families: YouTube options for your child.

Roblox: Parental Controls

Link your own Roblox account to your child's to manage their settings from your device and see their screen time and friends. On your child's account: Settings › Parental Controls › Add parent. Roblox asks you to verify your age with a government ID or credit card. If you don't want your own account, Roblox is rolling out a Parent mode that lets you approve requests on your child's device. Sources: Roblox Support: How to link your account and Parental Controls overview.

5. Test it on your child's phone

  • Try to open the app on your child's device. If it still opens, check you blocked it for the right child.
  • Try the website in Safari, since the app and the site are blocked separately.
  • Try reinstalling it from the App Store to make sure your install settings work.
  • Re-check after big iOS updates. Menu names change between versions, as they did in iOS 27.
