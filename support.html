This page is for KidWall 1.2. Keep KidWall up to date on your phone and on each kid's device.

Setting up

What do I need?
  • Your kid's iPhone or iPad with iOS 18 or later. It must be signed in with your kid's own child Apple Account in your Family Sharing group. Apple only allows parental-control apps on child accounts in a family. To make one, on your phone: Settings → Family → Add Member → Create Child Account. Apple's guide
  • Your own iPhone with iOS 18 or later, and your Apple Account password. You type it once on your kid's device to approve KidWall.
  • No other parental-control app approved on your kid's device. Apple allows only one.
  • KidWall Premium on your phone. It's free for a week, then you pay monthly or yearly. One subscription covers your Family Sharing group. Kid devices don't need it. If it ends, kid devices keep their rules, and you can still change them on the kid's device with the parent PIN.
  • Both devices online and signed in to iCloud, and about 10 minutes.
Order of setup

Set up your phone first, then your kid's device. Keep your kid's device with you until you've set the parent PIN.

On your phone:

  1. Install KidWall and open it. Tap This is my phone. Read Before you start and tap I'm ready.
  2. Start KidWall Premium with Start free week.
  3. On Make sure alerts reach you, tap Continue and allow notifications, so you hear when your kid asks for more time.
  4. Add a kid's device shows your family code and its QR code. Leave it open. You can find it again on the Family tab.

On your kid's device:

  1. Install KidWall and open it. Tap This is my kid's iPhone or iPad, check the list on Before you start, and tap Let's go.
  2. Step 1 of 6 — Turn on parental controls: tap Continue. When Apple asks, type your Apple Account password, not your kid's.
  3. Step 2 of 6 — Starting rules: bedtime, the daily limit, websites and Stop workarounds. Change anything you like, then tap Next. You can change all of it later from your phone.
  4. Step 3 of 6 — Name and parent PIN: type your kid's first name. If you like, give the device a name, such as "school iPad": your phone then shows it as "Emma's school iPad". Choose a PIN of 4 to 8 digits and type it twice. Don't use the device passcode your kid knows. Tap Turn on rules: the starting rules turn on now.
  5. Step 4 of 6 — Connect to your phone: tap Scan the code on your phone and point the camera at your phone. Or type the code under Or type it. The device shows up on your phone within a minute.
  6. Step 5 of 6 — Pick apps: choose what's Always blocked, what counts toward the daily limit, and what's Still allowed at bedtime. The daily limit counts only the apps you pick here. You can tap Skip for now.
  7. Step 6 of 6 — Instant changes: tap Continue and allow notifications, so a pause or a new rule arrives right away.
  8. The last screen shows what KidWall checked. Tap Done and hand the device to your kid.

If you stop after setting the PIN, open KidWall on the kid's device, tap Parent settings and type the PIN: setup goes on where you left off.

Apple ties app picks to the device they're made on, so apps can only be picked on your kid's device (Parent settings → Always blocked, Daily limit or Bedtime). Everything else, like the daily limit's minutes, bedtime hours, websites, location and pausing, can be changed later from your phone.

Finish setting up: the checklist

On your phone, each kid's card on the Family tab shows Finish setup and how many steps are done until setup is complete. Tap it for the checklist. It shows what KidWall checked on the kid's device, what to do there, and Apple settings that keep KidWall working, like a Screen Time passcode, Ask to Buy and KidWall in Always Allowed. Tap a step to see how to do it or fix it. Don't need the reminder? Turn off Show on the home screen at the bottom of the checklist, or touch and hold Finish setup and tap Hide.

On the kid's device, Parent settings starts with Setup on this device. It checks each step there (parental controls, connected to your phone, notifications and so on) and helps you fix anything that's off. Once everything is done it folds to Setup complete.

Approving KidWall on the kid's device didn't work

KidWall says what went wrong on Step 1, above the Continue button.

  • "This device isn't signed in with a child account in your Family Sharing." The name at the top of Settings on that device should be your kid's. To make a child account, on your phone: Settings → Family → Add Member → Create Child Account. If it's actually your own phone, tap This is my phone, go back.
  • "Another parental-control app is already approved here, and Apple allows only one." Remove the other app in Settings → Screen Time → Apps with Screen Time Access, then tap Continue.
  • "Connect to Wi-Fi and tap Continue." Apple's approval needs the device to be online.
  • "This device has restrictions that block parental-control apps, such as a school or work management profile." KidWall can't be turned on there. Use a device your family manages.
  • "No problem. Tap Continue when you're ready." The password prompt was closed. Tap Continue to try again.
  • "Apple couldn't turn on parental controls on this device." Check that it's online and signed in with your kid's own Apple Account, then tap Continue.

Chose This is my kid's iPhone or iPad on your own phone by mistake? Tap This is my phone, not my kid's, then Go back. This works only until KidWall is approved. On a kid's device that shows the KidWall Premium screen, tap This is my kid's device, not mine.

If your phone later says parental controls are off on a device, open KidWall on that device and tap Continue to turn them back on.

The kid's device isn't connected

On the kid's device, open Parent settings → Connect to parent's phone. It says what's wrong:

  • "Saved the code, but couldn't reach iCloud yet." Check Wi-Fi and tap Try again.
  • "This device isn't signed in to iCloud." Open Settings, sign in with your kid's Apple Account, and turn on KidWall under iCloud → Saved to iCloud. While Lock passcode & account changes is on, iOS won't let anyone sign in. Turn it off first under Parent settings → Stop workarounds, and turn it back on afterwards.

Codes look like ABCDE-FGHIJ-KLMNO-PQRST: 20 letters and numbers. Scanning the QR code avoids typos. Your code is on your phone under Add a kid's device, or More → Family code.

Naming a kid's device

A device name helps when a kid has more than one device. Give it one in Step 3 of setup, or later on the kid's device: Parent settings → Device name. Up to 24 characters, such as "school iPad". Your phone then shows "Emma's school iPad". Leave it empty to use the kid's name and the kind of device.

Everyday use

Finding your way around

KidWall on your phone has four tabs:

  • Family: a card for each kid's device, with what's going on now (Apps open, Paused, Bedtime until…, Limit reached, Extra time until…), and Pause and Extra time buttons. Requests for more time show at the top. Tap a card for your kid's page. Pull down to refresh.
  • Places: the places you get arrive and leave alerts for.
  • Activity: recent alerts, by day. Tap a kid's name at the top to see only theirs.
  • More: Add a kid's device, Family code, Help & guides, Privacy & your data and your subscription.

Your kid's page has four buttons: Pause (or Resume), Extra time, Locate and Rules. Under Today it shows bedtime, the daily limit, websites and location; tap one to change it. While the device shares its location, Location takes you to the map instead.

Changing rules

On your kid's page, tap Rules. The list shows each rule in one line: Daily limit, Bedtime, Websites, Stop workarounds, Location, Alerts and Name. Tap one to change it on its own page, then go back and tap Save. Cancel asks before throwing changes away.

When the kid's device has your changes, its page says "Emma's iPhone has your new rules." Until then it says it's waiting.

On the kid's device, Parent settings → Rules on this device has the same rules. Changes saved on your phone replace them, except the apps picked.

Pausing apps

Tap Pause on your kid's card, then Pause all apps, or tap Pause on your kid's page. Apps stay paused until you tap Resume. Apps allowed at bedtime still work while paused. Your phone tells you when the device is paused, or asks you to send it again if it couldn't check.

How fast do changes reach the kid's device?

Usually within a minute. Your phone sends each change to the kid's device as a notification, so keep KidWall's notifications allowed there. Setup on this device in the kid's Parent settings shows if they're off.

If a change doesn't get through (weak signal, notifications off), the kid's device checks in about every 8 minutes and catches up then. It has to be switched on and online. On the kid's device, Parent settings → Troubleshooting → Sync now fetches changes straight away.

Rule changes always catch up. Anything else you send (Pause, Resume, extra time, Locate now) that doesn't reach the device within 6 hours is dropped, so send it again once the device is back online. Extra time counts from when you send it, so if it reaches the device late, your kid gets only the minutes that are left, or none.

If a card says Not sure, the device hasn't checked in for a while. It may be off or offline.

A different bedtime each night

On your phone: your kid's page → Rules → Bedtime. Keep the switch on and choose Every night or Each night. Then go back and tap Save. Or on the kid's device: Parent settings → Bedtime.

  • Every night uses the same Starts and Ends times every night.
  • Each night lists the seven nights. Each night has its own switch and its own times, like a later bedtime on Friday and Saturday. Turn a night's switch off for no bedtime that night.

Each night starts on the day shown and runs into the next morning. Friday means Friday night into Saturday morning. So a Friday bedtime that starts after midnight, like 12:30 AM, starts early on Saturday.

Switching back to Every night keeps your nights for next time. Your kid's KidWall home screen shows tonight's bedtime.

A kid's device with KidWall older than 1.1.1 uses the Every night times. Your phone says so and shows Every night starts and Every night ends, so you can still set those.

My kid asked for more time

When your kid opens a blocked app, the block screen has an Ask for more time button. Tapping it sends your phone a notification, and a card at the top of the Family tab says your kid asks for more time. Tap +15 min, +30, +60 or No. The same card is on your kid's page.

You can also give time without being asked: tap Extra time on the card or your kid's page and pick the minutes. Extra time lasts that many minutes, so give it once the daily limit is used up or bedtime has started. Or, on your kid's page, tap Reset today's daily limit.

For those minutes, extra time lifts both the daily limit and bedtime. It doesn't unblock apps or sites under Always blocked, and it doesn't end a pause. To end a pause, tap Resume. While it's on, the card shows Extra time until and the time it ends.

When the minutes run out at bedtime, bedtime comes back on until it ends. If extra time is still running when bedtime starts, bedtime starts once the extra time is over.

Location and places

Sharing location or place alerts: what's the difference?

They're separate, and both are off until you turn them on for a device.

  • Share device location shows where the kid's device is on a map on your phone. Open your kid's page to see it, or tap Locate for a fresh location.
  • Place alerts (arrive & leave) send you an alert when the device arrives at or leaves a place you set up, like home or school. They work without sharing the location on your map.

To turn either one on, on your phone: your kid's page → Rules → Location, then go back and tap Save. Your kid can see on their KidWall home screen when their location is shared, and which places you get alerts about.

Location isn't showing

KidWall needs Always location permission on the kid's device. On the kid's device: Parent settings → Connect to parent's phone → Location permission. Allow "While Using", then tap the row again and choose "Change to Always Allow". If iOS has asked before, Settings opens instead: tap Location, then Always.

To stop your kid changing it, on your phone: Settings → Family → [kid] → Screen Time → Content & Privacy → Location Services → Don't Allow Changes. If the row says Restricted by Screen Time, set that to Allow Changes first, choose Always, then lock it again.

Setting up places

On your phone, open the Places tab and tap Add a place. Search for an address or tap the map. Or stand at the spot and tap Use my location: KidWall reads your phone's location once to drop the pin. Give the place a name and a Size (200 m, about 650 ft, or more is most reliable). Turn on Arrive, Leave or both, tick the devices under Watch on, and tap Save. Saving a new place turns on place alerts for the devices you ticked. Each device can watch up to 18 places.

On the kid's device, place alerts need Always location, Precise location on, and KidWall opened once after changing them. Low Power Mode and turning off Background App Refresh pause place alerts. Your phone shows that as a warning. To stop your kid turning off Background App Refresh: Screen Time → Content & Privacy → Background App Activities → Don't Allow Changes. Low Power Mode can't be locked.

Why did a place alert come late?

Alerts usually arrive within a few minutes, but they can be late or missed. That happens in Low Power Mode, without a network, or on an iPad without cellular away from Wi-Fi. Alerts also come a short distance past the edge of a place, not right at it. Don't rely on place alerts or the location map in an emergency.

Blocked websites

What does the web filter block?

On your phone: your kid's page → Rules → Websites. Or on the kid's device: Parent settings → Websites. It works in Safari and in apps that show web pages.

  • The first switch turns on Apple's website filter, the same one Screen Time uses, for websites that aren't safe for kids.
  • The social media switch blocks a built-in list of 17 social media websites.
  • Block other unsafe sites blocks a built-in list of 20 more websites that aren't safe for kids. It's on by default.
  • Hide other browsers hides browsers like Chrome and Firefox, so your kid can't use them to get around the filter.

Tap See the 17 sites or See the 20 sites to see every site on a list. Each site has its own switch. Turn one off to stop blocking just that site.

Blocking your own sites, and Always allowed

Under Sites you blocked, type a website address and tap Add. Tap a site to change it, or tap the red minus to remove it.

Under Always allowed, add sites Apple's filter blocks by mistake, like a school site. An always-allowed site works even if it's on a list above. Always allowed is used only while Apple's filter is on.

On your phone, go back to the rules list and tap Save to send your changes to the kid's device.

Why can I only block 49 sites?

Apple's web filter takes no more than 50 sites, so KidWall stops one short. Over 50, Apple's filter may block none of them. The 49 include the built-in lists. With both lists fully on, that's 37 sites, which leaves room for 12 of your own. To make room, turn off built-in sites you don't need, or remove some of yours. Always allowed has its own limit of 49.

If a device ever goes over the limit, the Websites page names the sites that aren't blocked.

Blocking a website or blocking an app

The web filter blocks websites, not apps. Blocking a website doesn't block the same company's app. To block an app, pick it on your kid's device: Parent settings → Always blocked → Apps, categories & sites. Apple ties app picks to the device they're made on, so you can't do this from your phone.

Help & guides

Game & chat safety

On your phone: More → Help & guides → Game & chat safety. There are guides for popular games and apps. Each one says what the game or app lets kids do, and where its own parent controls are.

KidWall can't read chats in any app, and no iPhone app can read chats inside another app. KidWall can block a game or app, or count it toward the daily limit. The game's own controls choose who your kid can talk to.

Help & guides also has What KidWall can and can't do, How KidWall works, the Apple settings that keep KidWall working, and Something's not working for common problems. On the kid's device, Parent settings → Help has the same guides; Something's not working is on your phone.

Second parent

Can both parents use KidWall?

Yes. Both phones use the same family code, and see the same kids, places and alerts. One KidWall Premium subscription covers your Family Sharing group.

On the other parent's iPhone: install KidWall and tap Other parent uses KidWall? Join with their code. Tap Scan the code on the other parent's phone and point it at your phone's More → Family code → Show QR code, or type the code and tap Join.

Anyone with this code can see alerts and change rules, so treat it like a password. Share it only with the other parent.

The other parent already set up KidWall with a different code

On the phone that should switch: More → Family code → Join the other parent's code…. Scan the code (it joins straight away), or type it and tap Join.

Switching only changes which family this phone sees. Nothing is deleted, and you can switch back under Go back to an earlier code. Kid devices stay on the code they were paired with. To move one, on that device: Parent settings → Connect to parent's phone → Unpair & delete this device's iCloud data, then connect again with the new code. Unpairing turns location sharing off and removes the device's places, so set those up again afterwards if you use them.

Parent PIN

I typed the wrong PIN too many times

The parent PIN protects Parent settings on the kid's device. Tap Parent settings at the bottom of the kid's KidWall home screen, type the PIN on the keypad and tap Unlock. The first 4 wrong tries in a row don't lock anything. After the 5th, KidWall makes you wait 1 minute before the next try. Each wrong try after that makes you wait longer: 5 minutes, then 15, then 60 minutes every time. The screen says "Too many wrong tries" and how many minutes are left. Closing KidWall doesn't end the wait. The right PIN resets the count.

I forgot the parent PIN

KidWall can't reset the PIN from your phone, and we can't either: KidWall keeps only a scrambled version of it, on the kid's device. Write it down somewhere your kid won't find it.

Without the PIN, you can still change rules, pause apps and give extra time from your phone. If you've lost it, email us and we'll help.

To change a PIN you know, on the kid's device: Parent settings → Change parent PIN.

Privacy & data

What can you see?

Nothing of your family's data. What your phones share goes through iCloud, encrypted with a key made from your family code before it leaves the phone. Without the code it's unreadable, to us and to Apple. If you add an ntfy address under Alerts, those alerts go to that service as plain text, without KidWall's encryption. iCloud can see when something was saved and a few plain details, like that a pause or extra time was sent and how many minutes. It can't see names, device names, rules, places, locations or what an alert says.

KidWall doesn't read messages or chats in any app, and it doesn't keep a history of the apps or websites your kid uses. An alert names an app or website only when your kid hits a block or asks for more time. Details are in the privacy policy.

Delete my data
  • On the kid's device: Parent settings → Connect to parent's phone → Unpair & delete this device's iCloud data. This deletes what that device stored in iCloud and turns location sharing off. It works without KidWall Premium.
  • On your phone, for one device: your kid's page → Remove this device & delete its data. If KidWall is still set up on that device, it shows up again the next time it checks in, so unpair it there first.
  • On your phone, for everything: More → Privacy & your data → Delete all family data & new family code. This deletes every device, rule, location and alert for your family, and makes a new code. Unpair the kid devices first: a device that's still paired keeps sending under the old code. If the other parent's phone uses the old code, switch it to the new one.
  • Deleting KidWall from a device removes everything it stored on that device.

The two options on your phone need KidWall Premium. Without it, your phone shows only the subscription screen, but unpairing on the kid's device still works.

Turning KidWall off

How do I turn everything off?

On the kid's device, open Parent settings → Turn off KidWall. Remove all restrictions turns everything off on that device, so KidWall can be deleted. It doesn't delete your rules. Rules come back when you tap Lock, open one of the rule pages there and leave it, open KidWall again, or save new rules for that device on your phone. While the device is paired, its next check-in can turn blocks back on too. So unpair first, and do it last.

To remove KidWall from a kid's device:

  1. On the kid's device: Parent settings → Connect to parent's phone → Unpair & delete this device's iCloud data. Then go back to Parent settings.
  2. Tap Turn off KidWall → Remove all restrictions. Don't tap Lock.
  3. Go to the Home Screen and delete KidWall. Because you approved KidWall during setup, iOS asks for your Apple Account password.

Deleting KidWall doesn't cancel KidWall Premium. To cancel, on your phone: Settings → your name → Subscriptions.
